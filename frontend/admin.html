Read this before you type anything

The database is MySQL, seeded from data.sql on startup. Anything you add here is live immediately — the map, the planner and the simulation all pick it up — and it persists across restarts.

To share your data with teammates or reproduce it on a clean machine: press Export data.sql, then replace backend/src/main/resources/data.sql with the download and commit it. That keeps the current state version-controlled and portable.

Never edit backend/target/classes/data.sql — that is generated output and is overwritten on the next build.

Stops

Leave id blank to create; fill it to overwrite an existing stop. Coordinates are decimal degrees — Yangon is around lat 16.8, lon 96.15.

🛣️

Routes

The fare is charged per boarding, so it is also what a transfer costs the rider — which is what makes transfers show up in the Pareto set.

🔢

Route stops

Builds a route's stop sequence. Saving replaces the whole itinerary, because sequence numbers and running times only make sense as a set.

🚦

Traffic prior

A factor of 1.0 means free-flow. Above 1.0 is slower, below is faster. DEFAULT applies to any segment without its own row; a specific segment is routeCode:fromStopId-toStopId. This drives both the ETA shown to riders and the speed of the buses on the map — change one and watch both move.

🧑‍✈️

Drivers and monthly scores

These numbers are the facts the Prolog rule bonus_eligible/2 reasons over: headway ≥ 80, harsh events ≤ 5, and no upheld complaint. Change one and the badge on the ops dashboard flips.

Buses

A new bus is placed on its route automatically — a bus dropped off its own line would jump the moment the simulator moved it. Crowding feeds the needs_seat constraint, so setting a bus to full changes what the rule base rules out.

⚠️

Incidents

Logged for the ops dashboard's counts. To actually withdraw journeys, use the flood button on the planner — that asserts flooded/1 into the rule base, which is a live condition rather than a historical record.
